A line of dialogue typed twice -- once in the screenplay, once in the breakdown --
drifts. The screenplay is rewritten, the breakdown is not, and a shot is
generated from last week's words without anyone being told.

So the screenplay says the words once, and a shot says which of them it holds,
by quoting the start of the line. Quoting survives rewrites elsewhere in the
scene, which a line number does not. A quote that is no longer there, or that
now matches two places, is an error: the link has to be fixed before the shot
is generated from it.

What the breakdown still owns is what the screenplay cannot say: how the line
is delivered, by which voice, where it sits in the mix. When the breakdown's
copy of a line differs from the screenplay, that is reported rather than
resolved, because either side can be the stale one. A line the screenplay does
not contain at all is only advice -- ad-libs are real.
