title = "Footage from outside carries where it came from"

A film may use pictures it did not make: an archive, a public library, stock.
What lets it show them is their licence, and what the licence usually asks
for is a credit. Both are known on the day the footage is chosen and forgotten
by the day the film is finished, so they are written where the shot is.

A shot whose picture is a stretch of a public file says so in its breakdown:
the file's address, the page that describes it, where the stretch starts and
how long it runs, its licence and its credit. The file itself is fetched, not
kept with the records: it is large, and anyone can fetch it again from what
the shot says. The take that results has the same facts beside it.

Footage with no address or no licence is an error, not advice. A film cut
around a clip nobody can account for has to be recut when someone asks.
