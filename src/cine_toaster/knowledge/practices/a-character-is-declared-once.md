A character described again in every scene is described differently in every
scene: on one production, one man had six descriptions across eight scenes.
Each reads well alone. The film does not.

So a character has one sheet. Scenes name them, and do not restate them. The
sheet holds the pictures a generation must use -- the master face -- and the
variants the story needs: the same person at fifteen and at thirty-five, or
before and after years in stasis. A scene says which variant it shows; it
never invents one.

A variant changes how someone looks; it does not change who they are. When a
variant needs another face -- years later, after stasis -- the sheet says why
(`face_changes:`). Otherwise two master faces for one person are two people,
and the audience will see two people.

A character generated without a master picture is a character the model makes
up again every time.
