title = "What a scene leaves behind holds in the next, unless the story changes it"
domain = "continuity"

A jacket taken off, a glass half drunk, a bandaged hand, a window left open:
whatever a scene ends on is still true when the film comes back to the same
people, unless time has passed or the story changes it on screen. Breaking
this without meaning to is the classic continuity error. The audience notices
before it can say what changed.

The script supervisor's ledger exists because no one remembers this across a
long shoot. It is truer still for generated shots, where each one is made
from scratch and nothing carries over by itself.

Only what the author declares is held to this: the scene's cast variant and
the facts listed under `continuity:`. Whether a scene follows the previous one
with no gap is the author's to say. Until it is said, a change in the same
place is asked about, not accused. A change the story wants is declared at
the shot where it happens, or accepted with its reason.
